PART 23 — LEGAL DOMAIN

The legal domain must provide structured support for standardized operational legal workflows while also clearly recognizing the boundary between automatable legal structure and matters requiring human legal review. It must operate with caution, documentation discipline, and permission awareness.

It must support:

NDA
MSA
SOW
service agreements
contractor agreements
privacy policies
disclaimers
DPA / data handling logic where applicable
compliance checklists
legal file storage
legal review status tracking

The system must also know when a matter exceeds safe automated boundaries and clearly indicate:

human legal review required

This domain must preserve:

traceability
review status
controlled access
document integrity
expiration and renewal awareness where relevant
legal record retention

Legal support must remain operationally useful but conservative. It must never pretend automated sufficiency where actual legal judgment is required.

client-facing QA checklists

Reporting must be designed as a core system layer, not as a final-stage add-on. The system must be able to explain itself continuously. Every major activity, state change, workflow, exception, and result must be reportable at an appropriate level of abstraction.

Every major activity must generate two reporting modes:

Executive / Leadership Report

Must be:

concise
decision-oriented
non-technical
outcome-focused
fast to read

It should explain:

what happened
what changed
what matters
where attention is needed
what decision may be needed next
Technical / Operational Report

Must be:

detailed
workflow-aware
traceable
operationally useful
more technical where appropriate

It should explain:

specific actions
workflow states
metrics
issues
performance
exceptions
next steps

This reporting discipline must apply across:

leads
campaigns
calls
meetings
CRM
production
execution
finance
legal
administration
quality
system health

Reporting is how the operating system becomes explainable, governable, and trustworthy.

Reports must not be shallow, generic, or mechanically summarized. Reporting must make the system intelligible. It must allow leadership, managers, and operators to understand not only that something occurred, but why it matters and what should happen next.

The reporting system must support:

role-sensitive reporting
leadership digest generation
technical operations logs
export and archive logic
Slack summaries

business consequence

readability by role

The operating system must be able to narrate its own activity at multiple layers of detail. Reporting should function as a cognitive compression layer that turns complexity into structured understanding.
