Mandatory understanding from the 35-part PMG design logic

You must understand and implement these foundational ideas:

Part 1 — Foundational Declaration

This is a new system from zero, not a redesign or inherited platform.

Part 2 — Core Product Identity

This is a unified AI-native business operating system, not separate tools.

Part 3 — Strategic Market Focus

The system is specifically for cybersecurity and IT companies.

Part 4 — Business Objective

The system must help PMG operate toward scalable growth and stronger control.

Part 5 — Intelligence Standard

Outputs must be elite, strategic, commercially useful, and non-generic.

Part 6 — AI-First Operating Philosophy

AI handles most structured work, humans handle high-risk and trust-heavy moments.

Part 7 — New-System Design Rule

Design the whole system before building modules.

Part 8 — The 11 Operating Domains

All 11 domains are true architectural layers.

Part 9 — Command Center

Must be executive nervous system, not a vanity dashboard.

Part 10 — Intelligence Domain

Must turn information into decision support and positioning advantage.

Part 11 — Outreach & Prospecting

Must generate qualified, context-rich pipeline.

Part 12 — Lead Intelligence Discipline

Must enforce quality over quantity and traceability.

Part 13 — Marketing & Discoverability

Must support real growth and channel logic.

Part 14 — Platform-Safe Multi-Channel Operation

Must protect platform safety, reputation, and operational discipline.

Part 15 — Production / Studio / Asset Generation

Must be a real production environment, not an output list.

Part 16 — Production Governance

Must enforce Generate → Preview → Review → Revise → Approve → Finalize.

Part 17 — Execution / Operations

Must make work happen and maintain continuity.

Part 18 — CRM / Sales / Revenue Pipeline

Must be a real revenue operating environment.

Part 19 — CRM Structure and Models

Must support PMG CRM, optional GHL, optional HubSpot-like patterns, and client CRM.

Part 20 — Communication Intelligence

Must support calls, meetings, summaries, coaching, and updates.

Part 21 — Human + AI Communication Coordination

AI must amplify human performance, not flatten it.

Part 22 — Finance Domain

Must support operational finance visibility and risk awareness.

Part 23 — Legal Domain

Must support legal docs and clearly flag human legal review when needed.

Part 24 — Administrative Domain

Must structure SOPs, forms, procedures, and internal documentation.

Part 25 — Quality Management

Must include QA gates, rejection reasons, and measurable review.

Part 26 — Reporting Domain

Must generate executive and technical reporting.

Part 27 — Reporting Style and Delivery

Must explain what happened, what matters, and what is next.

Part 28 — Archive / Documentation / Knowledge Memory

Must be structured, searchable, and operationally useful memory.

Part 29 — Slack / Communication Surface

Must act as a real reporting and communication layer.

Part 30 — System Core / Governance / Integrations / Infrastructure

Must function as the trust and control layer.

Part 31 — Permission Model

Permissions must change actual system behavior.

Part 32 — UI / UX Design System

Must be premium, cinematic, AI-native, dark, and controlled.

Part 33 — Responsive / Interaction / Accessibility

Must work across devices and support proper overlays, drawers, and state feedback.

Part 34 — Technology Stack / Delivery Standard

Must be modern, modular, scalable, maintainable, and production-grade.

Part 35 — Final Creation Mandate

No foundational layer may remain shallow.

Acceptance criteria for Phase 0

Do not proceed unless the codebase architecture is shaped around all of the above.
