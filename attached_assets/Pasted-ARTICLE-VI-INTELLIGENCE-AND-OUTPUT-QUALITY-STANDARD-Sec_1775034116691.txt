ARTICLE VI — INTELLIGENCE AND OUTPUT QUALITY STANDARD
Section 6.01 — General Standard

All AI-assisted or AI-operated behavior in the System must perform at a materially elevated intelligence threshold consistent with the Company’s directive that outputs reflect advanced reasoning, high precision, and serious business usefulness.

The intelligence standard shall include:

advanced contextual awareness
multi-step reasoning discipline
high signal density
specialized market relevance
commercial usefulness
precision in recommendations
disciplined generation behavior
low tolerance for shallow or generic outputs
Section 6.02 — Areas of Application

This intelligence standard shall apply across, without limitation:

market research
ICP development
company analysis
decision-maker discovery
pain-point identification
relevance and opportunity scoring
outreach logic
content and campaign planning
call guidance
meeting support
CRM routing
reporting synthesis
issue summarization
quality review assistance
operational next-step logic
leadership digest generation
Section 6.03 — Prohibited Output Behavior

The System shall not knowingly produce outputs that are:

generic without justification
fabricated in material respects
commercially empty
context-insensitive
strategically weak
overly verbose without signal
misleadingly certain
operationally unusable
blind to workflow state
blind to user role or permissions

Where confidence is limited, the System must preserve confidence awareness rather than simulate false certainty.

ARTICLE VII — AI-FIRST OPERATING PRINCIPLE WITH RESERVED HUMAN AUTHORITY
Section 7.01 — AI-First Leverage Model

The System shall be designed so that the majority of structured, repeatable, informational, coordination-heavy, and process-heavy work is performed through AI assistance, workflow automation, and orchestration logic.

The intended operating model broadly reflects:

AI as the primary carrier of repetitive operational burden
humans as the primary holders of judgment in high-consequence moments
Section 7.02 — Reserved Human Functions

Human authority shall remain required in matters involving:

negotiation
sensitive client communication
legal escalation
financial escalation
strategic override
relationship preservation
exception resolution
final approvals
final quality acceptance
other trust-heavy or high-risk decisions
Section 7.03 — AI Boundary Rule

No AI process shall be granted final autonomous authority where its output materially creates:

legal exposure
financial liability
external publishing risk
strategic misrepresentation
client trust damage
irreversible finalization without review

AI may recommend, prepare, summarize, route, sequence, and assist.
AI may not silently exceed safe operational boundaries.

ARTICLE VIII — HOLISTIC DESIGN DUTY
Section 8.01 — System-First Requirement

The System must be conceived and designed holistically before being implemented modularly.

No domain may be built as though it exists independently from:

reporting
permissions
archive
approval pathways
integrations
role responsibilities
cross-domain data dependencies
executive visibility requirements
Section 8.02 — Prohibited Design Approach

The following implementation tendencies are expressly disfavored and, where material, prohibited:

building disconnected screens and linking them later
treating CRUD modules as equivalent to enterprise operating capability
introducing workflows without defined states
introducing states without reporting implications
introducing records without ownership
introducing approvals without traceability
introducing AI without context boundaries
Section 8.03 — Required Pre-Implementation Definitions

Before material implementation of any major domain, the following shall be defined:

objective and business purpose
owned entities and records
state models
permissions behavior
approval and rejection logic
reporting outputs
archive and retention behavior
integration points
risk conditions
escalation conditions
AI participation boundaries
ARTICLE IX — DOMAIN CONSTITUTION AND ARCHITECTURAL ORGANIZATION
Section 9.01 — Eleven Operating Domains

The System shall be organized into eleven formal operating domains:

Command Center & Executive Control
Intelligence, Market Research & Strategic Insight
Outreach, Prospecting & Pipeline Discovery
Marketing, SEO, Brand Awareness & Discoverability
Production, Studio, Brand & Asset Generation
Execution, Operations, Publishing & Workflow Control
CRM, Sales, Closing & Revenue Pipeline
Communication Intelligence, Calling & Meeting Support
Finance, Legal, Administrative & Quality Management
Reporting, Archive, Documentation & Knowledge Memory
System Core, Governance, Permissions, Integrations & Infrastructure
Section 9.02 — Mandatory Characteristics of Each Domain

Each domain must define, at minimum:

domain objective
scope boundaries
user roles
records and entities
workflow definitions
lifecycle states
AI participation
approval conditions
reporting duties
archive responsibilities
integration relationships
business outcomes
exception conditions
supervision requirements
Section 9.03 — Domain Interoperability Requirement

Domains must be structurally distinct but operationally interoperable.
No domain shall become a silo where information cannot flow, reports cannot explain activity, or workflows lose continuity.

The purpose of domain separation is architectural integrity, not fragmentation.

ARTICLE X — COMMAND, VISIBILITY, AND EXECUTIVE CONTROL
Section 10.01 — Executive Oversight Requirement

The Command Center shall function as the genuine top-level oversight surface of the System and shall not be reduced to a decorative dashboard.

It must provide authorized visibility into, at minimum:

pipeline health
lead quality
campaign status
production queues
pending approvals
calls and meetings
CRM movement
delivery progression
finance summaries
quality issues
archive activity
system health
risk signals
intervention queues
Section 10.02 — Mandatory Questions the System Must Answer

The executive control layer must allow authorized stakeholders to determine:

what is happening
what matters most
what is behind
what is blocked
what is risky
where human attention is needed
what requires decision
what must happen next

A control system that cannot answer these questions shall be deemed inadequate.

ARTICLE XI — COMMERCIAL WORKFLOW, CLIENT TRUST, AND COMMUNICATION GOVERNANCE
Section 11.01 — Revenue Workflow Integrity

The System must preserve continuity from intelligence to outreach, from outreach to qualification, from qualification to CRM, from CRM to meeting, from meeting to proposal, and from proposal to close and post-close operational handoff.

Any architectural gap that materially breaks this continuity shall be treated as a defect of enterprise significance.

Section 11.02 — Communication Support Principle

The communication domain must improve human effectiveness, consistency, context awareness, and follow-through, whether through AI-led workflows, human-led workflows with AI guidance, or human-led meetings supported by AI summarization and recommendation logic.

The System shall not reduce communication intelligence to mere transcription. It must support situational awareness, objection handling, next-step logic, and CRM continuity.

Section 11.03 — Trust Preservation Rule

Because the Company operates in credibility-heavy and trust-sensitive commercial environments, the System must preserve restraint, clarity, contextual accuracy, and quality in all communication-related workflows.

Volume without trust shall not qualify as success.

ARTICLE XII — FINANCE, LEGAL, ADMINISTRATIVE, AND QUALITY DUTIES
Section 12.01 — Finance

The System shall support meaningful operational finance visibility, including but not limited to quotes, invoices, receipts, payment tracking, profitability awareness, expense awareness, outstanding balances, and tax-ready export support.

The finance domain must support real operational awareness rather than symbolic bookkeeping.

Section 12.02 — Legal

The legal domain must support structured handling of NDAs, MSAs, SOWs, service agreements, contractor agreements, privacy policies, disclaimers, compliance checklists, and legal review status.

Where matters exceed templated or safe automated boundaries, the System must clearly designate:

human legal review required

Section 12.03 — Administrative Integrity

The System shall maintain administrative materials such as SOPs, procedures, forms, policy documents, instructions, memos, handover records, and escalation forms in a structured and retrievable way.

Administrative clarity is part of operational maturity and not optional overhead.

Section 12.04 — Quality Management

Quality must be treated as an explicit system layer. Review rules, approval gates, rejection reasons, issue logging, revision traceability, and final acceptance checkpoints must be materially supported.

Outputs that cannot be reviewed, rejected, or improved in a structured manner shall not be considered governed outputs.
