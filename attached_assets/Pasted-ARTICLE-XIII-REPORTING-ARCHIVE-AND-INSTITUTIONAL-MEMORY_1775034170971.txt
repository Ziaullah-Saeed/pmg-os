ARTICLE XIII — REPORTING, ARCHIVE, AND INSTITUTIONAL MEMORY
Section 13.01 — Reporting as Core Infrastructure

Reporting must exist as a first-class architectural layer and may not be deferred as a late-stage cosmetic feature.

The System must support both:

executive / leadership reporting
technical / operational reporting

for all major domains and material workflows.

Section 13.02 — Reporting Duty

Reports must explain:

what happened
what changed
what matters
what requires attention
what next decision or action is indicated

Reporting that merely restates raw activity without interpretive value shall be considered insufficient.

Section 13.03 — Archive and Memory Duty

The System shall preserve searchable, permission-aware, categorized business memory across contracts, SOPs, finance records, proposals, call logs, meeting summaries, CRM history, quality records, reports, assets, and internal documents.

The archive must function as durable institutional memory, not as an unstructured storage bucket.

ARTICLE XIV — PERMISSIONS, GOVERNANCE, AND CONTROL INFRASTRUCTURE
Section 14.01 — Permission Levels

The System shall enforce, at minimum, four principal permission levels:

Super Admin
Admin
Manager
User
Section 14.02 — Behavioral Consequences of Permissions

Permissions must shape actual system behavior, including but not limited to:

page access
module access
action rights
approval rights
publishing authority
financial visibility
archive visibility
reporting visibility
system controls
governance settings
integration control

Permissions that only hide interface elements, while leaving business behavior materially uncontrolled, shall be insufficient.

Section 14.03 — Core Governance Requirements

The System Core domain must provide:

secrets management
environment control
integration supervision
audit trails
backup logic
recovery awareness
dependency visibility
incident awareness
alerting
operational supervision

This is the trust layer of the System and must be treated accordingly.

ARTICLE XV — UX, PRESENTATION, AND ENTERPRISE OPERABILITY
Section 15.01 — Interface Standard

The System must present itself as an advanced AI-native operating environment characterized by premium control, clarity, authority, and reduced cognitive burden.

The experience must feel:

premium
controlled
minimal but powerful
futuristic where appropriate
serious enough for enterprise reliance
Section 15.02 — Responsiveness and Accessibility

The System must function credibly across desktop, laptop, tablet, and mobile environments, with proper layout adaptation, touch sensitivity where relevant, readable hierarchy, controlled modal behavior, and accessibility-conscious interactions.

A visually attractive interface that fails under operational use shall not meet constitutional standards.

ARTICLE XVI — TECHNOLOGY, DELIVERY, AND READINESS
Section 16.01 — Technology Standard

The System shall be built using modern, enterprise-capable technologies consistent with the underlying directive, including the identified frontend, state, design, AI, and backend patterns stated in the source specification.

Technology choices must support:

production readiness
security
maintainability
modularity
observability
scalability
controlled iteration
Section 16.02 — Delivery Standard

The Company expressly rejects delivery of a system that is nominally complete but operationally fragile. The required standard is that the platform be:

production-grade
stable
supervisable
reportable
auditable
maintainable
credible for real business use

Basic broken flows, weak role handling, hidden errors, and incomplete governance are not acceptable as normal post-launch conditions.

ARTICLE XVII — NON-NEGOTIABLE FINAL MANDATE
Section 17.01 — Foundational Completeness Requirement

No foundational layer may be left materially shallow if it belongs to the operating system core. This includes, without limitation:

reporting
archive
permissions
governance
workflow continuity
AI quality
operational visibility
approval structures
role-based control
system supervision
Section 17.02 — Prohibited End State

The System must not conclude as:

a prototype
a superficial dashboard
a collection of disconnected tools
a partially intelligent app
a collection of loosely governed modules
a visually strong but operationally weak platform
Section 17.03 — Required End State

The required end state is:

A complete AI-powered company operating inside software.

This phrase shall be understood as the governing outcome standard for the initiative. The System must be sufficiently integrated, intelligent, controlled, traceable, reportable, and operationally serious to deserve that description.
